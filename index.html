The Program

Train all year.
Compete on your own stage.

The Afro-Academic, Cultural, Technological and Scientific Olympics is the NAACP’s yearlong achievement program for high school students — founded in 1978 on a simple conviction: that young people who excel in a laboratory or on a page deserve the same recognition we give athletes.

Students don’t just show up and perform. They spend the school year working with mentors, coaches, and working artists, building a piece or a project to competition standard. In March, they take the stage in Lafayette. Local gold medalists advance to the national ACT-SO competition, where national gold medalists earn scholarships.

Open to high school students in grades 9–12 who are U.S. citizens and amateurs in their category. There is a place here for you.

Vocal and instrumental music each compete in classical and contemporary; dance in ballet, contemporary, modern, and cultural — twenty categories in all. ACT-SO runs 33 nationally; STEM, business, and culinary open here as student interest and mentors surface — tell us what you want to compete in.

The Path

From sign-up to the national stage.

01

Fall 2026

Register & pair with a mentor

Students sign up and are matched with a mentor or coach in their category. Families get a full orientation before any of the work begins.

02

Winter

Train through the school year

Workshops and coaching sessions run from late fall into spring: real craft, real feedback, and a piece or project built to compete.

03

March 13, 2027

The Lafayette competition

Judged by degreed and working professionals in each category, on ACT-SO’s 100-point national standard. Gold, silver, and bronze medals awarded.

04

July 2027

Nationals

Local gold medalists represent Lafayette at the national ACT-SO competition. National gold medalists earn scholarships.

Student Sign-Up

Put your name in early.

Official registration follows NAACP rules this fall. Signing up here puts your student on the roster list and gets your family the orientation details first. This goes straight to the program team and nowhere else.

Mentors, Judges & Sponsors

This runs on people who know how to do.

Every category needs a panel of three judges — degreed or working professionals in the field. Every student needs a coach. And every line of this program, from workshops to medals to sending our gold medalists to nationals, can carry a name.
